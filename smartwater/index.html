Chapter I

The long way to your glass.

01

It starts in a lake or an aquifer.

Rain runs off roads, farms and rooftops into the reservoirs that supply North Texas. Along the way it collects fertilizer runoff, industrial residue and natural minerals like calcium and magnesium.

02

The treatment plant makes it legal.

Cities add chlorine or chloramine to kill bacteria, which is essential. But those disinfectants react with organic matter and create byproducts like TTHMs and haloacetic acids. Legal limits for many of these were set decades ago.

03

Then it travels miles of aging pipe.

Water mains across the country are decades old. Corrosion, sediment and, in older neighborhoods, lead service lines can add what the treatment plant just took out.

04

Finally, it reaches your family.

Into the coffee maker. The baby bottle. The shower your kids stand in every night. Nobody tests it again after it leaves the plant. Unless you do.

Chapter IV

What you can't see, you can feel.

North Texas water is naturally hard. The same white crust on your showerhead is forming inside your water heater, dishwasher, washing machine and every pipe in the walls.

  • Spots & film on glasses and shower doors
  • Dry, itchy skin and dull, brittle hair
  • Chlorine smell or "pool" taste from the tap
  • Shorter life for water heaters and appliances
  • Higher bills as scale insulates heating elements
